The operational reality

Small process gaps create unnecessary work.

We start by understanding how work moves through your team. These are examples of friction points we can investigate—not assumptions about every business.

01 / INTAKE

Enquiries across too many channels

Requests arrive through calls, forms and messaging apps. Important details can become scattered or need to be entered more than once.

02 / COORDINATION

Manual appointment handoffs

Availability checks, confirmations and internal updates can involve repeated calls and avoidable back-and-forth between staff.

03 / FOLLOW-UP

Requests that are hard to track

When ownership and next steps are unclear, staff may struggle to see which enquiry or customer request still needs attention.

What we build

Software first. AI where it is useful.

We connect the tools and steps your team already uses. We prefer clear rules and dependable integrations; AI is introduced selectively for work involving unstructured information.

SYSTEM 01

Intake and structured records

Capture information from agreed forms or channels, check required fields and route approved records into an authorized spreadsheet, database or CRM.

Request→Validation→Record
SYSTEM 02

Notifications and follow-up

Coordinate internal handoffs, notify the right person and make pending work easier to review, with clear ownership and fallback steps.

New item→Assigned owner→Reminder
SYSTEM 03

Software and system integrations

Connect existing tools through supported APIs and webhooks where access, permissions and the customer's systems make it appropriate.

Existing tools→Integration→Shared workflow
SYSTEM 04

Carefully applied AI

Text→Schema check→Human review
Our engineering method

Clarity before code. Verification before handover.

Every engagement begins with the real process—not a preselected tool or an assumption that AI is required.

Understand

Map the current workflow, people involved, tools used and points where work slows down.

Define scope

Agree on one bounded problem, requirements, exclusions and practical success measures.

Build carefully

Implement the smallest suitable solution, using test data and controlled access.

Test and review

Check expected cases, failure paths and human approval points before release.

Handover

Document the workflow, explain its operation and define a manual fallback.

Built with care

Useful automation must remain understandable and controllable.

Reliability is a design and operating practice—not a badge or a guarantee. The controls used in each project depend on its actual data, systems and risk.

01

Human oversight for critical steps

External messages and sensitive actions can be held for authorized staff approval. Unclear inputs should move to a review path.

02

Data minimization

Design around the minimum information needed for the agreed workflow, with access limited to authorized people and systems.

03

Synthetic-data demonstrations

Early demonstrations and pre-release tests use fictional records rather than real patient or customer information.

04

Failure paths and manual fallback

Timeouts, invalid data and unavailable integrations should be visible, with a documented way for staff to continue work.

Clear boundary: Avenmark AI does not provide medical diagnosis, interpret clinical reports or replace a laboratory information system. Any handling of sensitive information or live patient workflows requires a separate review of permissions, safeguards and applicable obligations before implementation.

A focused first engagement

The 14-Day Workflow Improvement Pilot

A fixed-scope engagement to investigate one repetitive operational bottleneck, implement a suitable workflow improvement and review what changed. No ongoing contract is required to begin a pilot.

  • One agreed intake, coordination or follow-up workflow
  • Written scope, dependencies and acceptance criteria
  • Integration with an approved tool where access is available
  • Testing, staff walkthrough and manual fallback notes
  • Outcome review against measures agreed at the outset

Is a pilot the right starting point?

We begin with a short discovery conversation. If the problem is suitable, we define the scope and prerequisites before confirming the schedule. The 14-day period starts only after those dependencies are ready.

Discuss Pilot Fit ↗

No guaranteed savings or outcomes are claimed. Measures and feasibility are agreed based on the workflow and available baseline information.

A sensible fit matters

Start with a problem that can be clearly bounded.

We would rather identify a good fit—or say when we are not the right fit—than promise a broad transformation without understanding the work.

A good starting point

  • A repetitive administrative workflow takes staff time.
  • There is a clear process owner who can explain and review it.
  • The relevant tools provide an approved way to integrate.
  • The first improvement can be tested within a limited scope.

Not what this pilot is for

  • Autonomous clinical decisions or medical diagnosis.
  • Replacing an entire core system in two weeks.
  • Unapproved access to patient records or third-party platforms.
  • Guaranteed ROI before the workflow and baseline are understood.
